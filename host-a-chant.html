HOW A GATHERING GOES

First hour
We arrive

People come in, get tea, and settle. We start with something simple that everyone can sing straight away.

The middle
We learn a few chants

We hand out lyric sheets and I teach the chants by ear. The room slowly eases into rhythmic vibrations.

Afterwards
We stop trying

At some point we fall into a simple resonance and presence in sound and in silence. It's nourishing in ways many of us have forgotten.

Let's talk Tell me about your room and I'll tell you if it works.

When it clicks — when a room full of people is singing together — you can feel it in your body. Something is happening here, right now, and we are all part of it.

Hasan Can Tutal

What a host provides

An undisturbed space for singing
Chairs or cushions, in a circle
Twelve people or more
Tea, or something to drink
A donation pot, or a fee we agree beforehand

Friends, neighbours, whoever you'd like there. No piano, no sound system, no chairs facing forward — the circle is the only part of the setup that matters.

What a gathering isn't

It isn't a concert, so nobody sits and watches. It isn't a workshop, so there's nothing to get right. And it isn't a choir rehearsal — we're not working towards a performance, and nothing leaves the room unless everyone wants it to.

About Nathan
